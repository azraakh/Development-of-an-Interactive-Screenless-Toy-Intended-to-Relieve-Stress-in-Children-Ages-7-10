# Development-of-an-Interactive-Screenless-Toy-Intended-to-Relieve-Stress-in-Children-Ages-7-10
Many children encounter stress and uneasiness at school, home, and in social settings. While digital based interventions can offer distraction, they may increase dependency on tablets and phones which affects the child’s growth negatively. In this regard, non-digital based interventions can suggest an alternative solution. To design and prototype a non-digital interactive electronic based toy that combines tactile engagement with soothing LED patterns through designed games which can redirect the child’s attention during stressful moments. This project uses a human-centered design approach, focusing on elementary school aged children. Early research analyzed different toys, including mechanical Tetris-inspired puzzles, fidget balls, water ball mazes and warm, light emitting plush toys. Based on the design concepts, the leading concept is chosen to be a spherical shaped electronic toy. The design process included sketches, material selection and 3D printing the parts. The built toy consists of mini spheres that have LEDs, buttons and magnetic connections and a greater sphere that serves as the control unit housing a microcontroller. The mini spheres can be played individually. When connected to the control unit, spheres are designed to display soothing LED patterns that children can interact with. The toy development process will continue, focusing on sourcing materials for the exterior and developing various soothing LED and button-based interactions. Making the spheres independent will be investigated, and user feedback from educators, parents and children will be obtained. The project is expected to have a refined final version that demonstrates results of reduced stress. The final design will prioritize comfort, simplicity and safety. This project proposes a child-centered design comfort toy combining soothing LED patterns and tactile engagement. The work contributes to early stage design research on playful non-digital devices for stress redistraction in children.
